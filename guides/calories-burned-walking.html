Calories Burned Walking

How many calories walking really burns, how pace, weight and hills change it, and how to use walking for weight loss.

Walking is the most accessible exercise there is, and it burns more calories than most people expect, especially done briskly and often. It is also easy to fit into a day without a gym or special equipment. This guide explains how the numbers work, what changes them, and how to turn walking into a real part of a weight plan.

How the maths works

Calories burned walking depend on your weight, your pace, and how long you walk. A rough rule is that walking burns about 0.5 calories per kilogram of body weight per kilometre at a moderate pace, or about 3.5 calories per minute for a 70 kg person walking briskly. Using the standard MET formula, moderate walking has a MET value of about 3.5, so a 70 kg person burns about 3.5 times 70, or 245 calories, in an hour. A 90 kg person walking the same hour burns about 315 calories, because a heavier body costs more energy to move.

Pace changes everything

A slow stroll burns noticeably less than a brisk walk. At a gentle pace of about 3 km per hour, the MET value is around 2.5, while a brisk pace of 6 km per hour is around 4.3, and a very fast walk of 7 km per hour or more is around 5. Hills add more: walking uphill can raise the burn by 30 to 50 percent compared with flat ground. Carrying a load, such as a backpack or shopping bags, adds further. The practical lesson is that walking faster, not just longer, is the quickest way to burn more.

Worked example: 10,000 steps

Ten thousand steps is roughly 7 to 8 kilometres for most adults, and takes about 90 minutes of walking in total across the day. For a 70 kg person walking at a moderate pace, that is roughly 350 to 400 calories. For a 90 kg person, it is roughly 450 to 500. That is a meaningful contribution to a weight-loss deficit, and it comes without the hunger that hard exercise often brings. It also explains why a daily step target is such a popular weight-loss habit: it is measurable, repeatable, and hard to fake.

Walking for weight loss

Walking alone rarely produces fast weight loss, because the burn per hour is modest, but it is one of the most sustainable ways to create a deficit. A daily 45-minute brisk walk burns roughly 200 to 250 calories for most adults, which over a week is 1,400 to 1,750 calories, or about 0.2 kg of fat. Combined with a modest reduction in food, that adds up to a steady loss without hunger or exhaustion. Walking after meals also helps blood sugar, and walking outdoors adds the benefits of daylight and fresh air.

Making it a habit

The best walking plan is the one that fits your day. Walk part of the way to work, take the stairs, walk during phone calls, or add a 20-minute walk after lunch and dinner. A step tracker or phone app helps, because it turns an invisible habit into a visible number. Aim for a step target you can hit most days rather than an ambitious one you hit twice a week. Consistency beats intensity with walking, and a daily habit of 8,000 to 10,000 steps is a realistic goal for most people.

A week of walking, added up

Take a 75 kg person who adds a 45-minute brisk walk to each day. At a moderate pace, the walk burns about 200 calories, so the week adds about 1,400 calories of extra burn, roughly 0.2 kg of fat. Add a second walk at the weekend, or take the stairs instead of the lift, and the week reaches 1,800 calories. Over a month, that is about 0.8 kg, and over six months about 5 kg, all from walking, without changing food at all. The numbers are modest week by week, which is why walking feels slow, but they are large over months, which is why walking works. The key is the daily habit: a walk you do every day beats a long walk you do once a week, because the total is what matters, not the single best day.

Tip: A 70 kg person burns about 245 calories in an hour of brisk walking — and 10,000 steps a day is roughly 350 to 400 calories, a meaningful share of a weight-loss deficit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does walking burn?

About 3.5 calories a minute for a 70 kg person walking briskly, or roughly 245 calories an hour. Heavier people burn more.

Is 10,000 steps a day enough for weight loss?

It helps a lot: roughly 350 to 400 calories a day for most adults. Combined with a modest food reduction, it produces steady loss.

Does walking speed matter?

Yes. A brisk walk burns far more than a slow stroll, and hills add 30 to 50 percent more.
