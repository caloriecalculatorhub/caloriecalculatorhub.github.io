Calorie Needs for Men

How many calories men typically need, what drives the number, and how activity and muscle change it.

Men typically need more calories than women of the same age and height, mainly because they usually have more muscle mass and a larger body size. The difference is an average, not a rule: a small, sedentary man may need fewer calories than a tall, active woman. This guide explains the typical ranges for men, what pushes the number up or down, and how to find your own figure.

Typical ranges

A moderately active man aged 19 to 30 may need around 2,600 to 2,800 calories a day. In the 30s and 40s, that falls to roughly 2,400 to 2,600, and after 50 to around 2,200 to 2,400. Sedentary men need less: roughly 2,200 to 2,400 in their 20s, falling to about 2,000 by their 60s. Very active men, including manual workers and athletes, may need 3,000 or more. These are broad averages from dietary guidance, and your own number depends on your weight, height and activity, so use the calculator for a personal estimate.

Why men need more on average

The main reason is body composition. Muscle burns more energy at rest than fat, and men on average carry more muscle and less body fat than women of the same weight. Men are also on average taller and heavier, which raises the energy needed to maintain the body. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation reflects this with a different constant for men: plus 5 instead of minus 161, a difference of 166 calories a day for people of the same age, weight and height. That difference is an average, and individual men vary widely around it.

Muscle and activity matter most

Two men of the same weight can have daily needs hundreds of calories apart. The one with more muscle has a higher resting metabolism, and the one with a physical job or a daily training habit burns far more through activity. Strength training is the most effective way to raise your calorie needs over time, because it builds the muscle that burns energy at rest. Daily steps matter too: a desk worker who walks 10,000 steps a day burns roughly 300 to 400 calories more than one who walks 3,000.

Weight loss for men

Men often lose weight faster than women at first, because a higher starting weight means a higher calorie burn, and a 500-calorie deficit is a smaller share of a larger total. A moderately active man with a maintenance level of 2,600 calories would aim for about 2,100 a day to lose roughly 0.5 kg a week. Avoid very low intakes: below about 1,500 calories a day for men should only be followed with medical supervision. Keep protein high during weight loss, around 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight, to protect muscle.

Common pitfalls

The most common error is overestimating activity: a man who trains three times a week but sits the rest of the day is moderately active, not very active, and choosing the higher level adds 300 or more calories to the estimate. Alcohol is another: a few drinks can add 300 to 500 calories without any feeling of fullness. Large portions are a third pitfall, because restaurant and takeaway servings are often far bigger than a standard portion. Weighing food for a week is the fastest way to see where the extra calories are coming from.

A worked example for a 45-year-old man

Take a 45-year-old man who weighs 90 kg, is 180 cm tall, and works at a desk but trains three times a week. His BMR is 10 times 90, or 900, plus 6.25 times 180, or 1,125, minus 5 times 45, or 225, plus 5, which gives 1,805 calories. At a moderate activity factor of 1.55, his daily needs are about 2,798 calories. If he wants to lose 0.5 kg a week, he aims for about 2,300 calories. If he changes to a sedentary job and stops training, his multiplier falls to 1.2 and his needs fall to about 2,166, a drop of more than 600 calories. The example shows how much activity matters, and why a change in routine should always be followed by a recalculation.

Tip: The formula gives men 166 calories a day more than women of the same age, weight and height — an average difference in muscle mass, not a rule about individuals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does the average man need?

A moderately active man in his 20s to 40s typically needs around 2,400 to 2,800 a day, falling with age. Sedentary men need less; very active men need more.

Why do men need more calories than women?

On average, more muscle mass and larger body size. Muscle burns more energy at rest than fat.

How many calories should a man eat to lose weight?

About 500 below maintenance, for roughly 0.5 kg a week. Avoid going below about 1,500 calories a day without medical advice.
