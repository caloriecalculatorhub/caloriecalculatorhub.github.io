Calorie Needs for Women

How many calories women typically need, how the number changes through life, and how to find your own figure.

Women typically need fewer calories than men of the same age and height, mainly because of differences in average body size and muscle mass. The number also changes more across life stages, with pregnancy, breastfeeding and menopause each shifting needs in different directions. This guide explains the typical ranges, what changes them, and how to find a personal figure rather than relying on a generic average.

Typical ranges

A moderately active woman aged 19 to 30 may need around 2,000 to 2,200 calories a day. In the 30s and 40s, that falls to roughly 1,900 to 2,100, and after 50 to around 1,800 to 2,000. Sedentary women need less: roughly 1,800 in their 20s, falling to about 1,600 by their 60s. Very active women may need 2,400 or more. These are broad averages, and your own number depends on your weight, height, muscle mass and activity, so use the calculator for a personal estimate rather than taking the average as your target.

How the number changes through life

Pregnancy raises calorie needs, but less than many people expect: roughly an extra 300 calories a day in the second and third trimesters, not double. Breastfeeding raises needs more, by roughly 300 to 500 calories a day, because producing milk costs energy. Menopause often lowers needs, because muscle mass declines and activity may drop, which is why many women gain weight at this stage without eating more. The answer is not a drastic diet but a small adjustment, plus strength training to protect muscle.

Muscle and activity matter most

Two women of the same weight can have daily needs hundreds of calories apart. The one with more muscle has a higher resting metabolism, and the one who walks 10,000 steps a day burns roughly 300 calories more than one who walks 3,000. Strength training twice a week is the most effective way to keep calorie needs up over time, because it preserves the muscle that burns energy at rest. Daily movement matters as much as formal exercise, and it is often the bigger share of the difference.

Weight loss for women

A moderately active woman with a maintenance level of 2,000 calories would aim for about 1,500 a day to lose roughly 0.5 kg a week. Avoid very low intakes: below about 1,200 calories a day should only be followed with medical supervision, because it is hard to get enough nutrients and it often costs muscle. Keep protein high during weight loss, around 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight, and be patient: weight can fluctuate by a kilogram or more across the menstrual cycle with water retention, so judge progress by the monthly trend, not a single reading.

Common pitfalls

The most common error is choosing too high an activity level: three workouts a week plus a desk job is moderately active, not very active. Liquid calories are another: a milky coffee, a smoothie or a glass of juice can add 100 to 200 calories without feeling like food. Weekend eating is a third pitfall: a careful weekday diet can be undone by two days of large meals and drinks. Tracking for a week, including the weekend, is the fastest way to see the real picture.

A worked example for a 35-year-old woman

Take a 35-year-old woman who weighs 65 kg, is 165 cm tall, and exercises moderately three times a week. Her BMR is 10 times 65, or 650, plus 6.25 times 165, or 1,031, minus 5 times 35, or 175, minus 161, which gives 1,345 calories. At a moderate activity factor of 1.55, her daily needs are about 2,085 calories. To lose 0.5 kg a week, she aims for about 1,585 calories. During pregnancy, her needs would rise by about 300 calories a day in the later trimesters, and while breastfeeding by about 300 to 500 a day. After menopause, her needs may fall by 100 to 200 calories as muscle mass declines, which is why strength training and a small dietary adjustment matter at that stage.

Tip: Pregnancy adds roughly 300 calories a day in the later trimesters, and breastfeeding about 300 to 500 — far less than the old idea of eating for two.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does the average woman need?

A moderately active woman in her 20s to 40s typically needs around 1,900 to 2,200 a day, falling with age. Sedentary women need less; very active women need more.

How do calorie needs change in pregnancy?

By roughly an extra 300 calories a day in the second and third trimesters. Breastfeeding adds roughly 300 to 500 a day.

How many calories should a woman eat to lose weight?

About 500 below maintenance, for roughly 0.5 kg a week. Avoid going below about 1,200 calories a day without medical advice.
